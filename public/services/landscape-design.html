01 / 03

Bed shapes, layouts and plant picks

Every layout takes its cue from the house. Beds follow the line of the walk, the drive and the front door, and curves tend to win out because they break up large stucco walls and stone columns. The entry bed matters most since it’s what visitors notice first, and paver-style curbing can hold that curve clean.

Then the plant list. This climate suits crotons, hibiscus, foxtail ferns, agave, small palms, bromeliads and tropicals like heliconia or canna. Each plant is placed in its nursery pot first, so you can approve the spacing while it’s easy to change.

02 / 03

Can it handle the sun and salt?

Putting the right plant in the right spot matters most. A shade plant beside a sunny driveway will cook, and salty air near the water scorches tender leaves. Sandy soil drains quickly, so whatever goes in has to ride out dry stretches between storms.

UF/IFAS teaches the same idea through Florida-Friendly Landscaping: match each plant to its spot and group plants by thirst. Beds built that way use less water, ride out both the wet summer and the dry spring, and fill in without crowding.

03 / 03

From first visit to finished bed

Your free estimate happens on foot. Point out what bugs you and what you’d like more or less of, and the crew takes measurements while talking through beds, plants and drip lines. They’ll also note where sun and shade fall, the sprinklers already there and any HOA limits.

Install day goes in order: old material out, beds shaped, plants in, plus a drip line through the bed if needed. The mulch or rock finish comes last, and then the crew sweeps up before leaving.

Do I need a design drawn up first?

Not usually. For most yards, a walk around and a chat do the job. Share what you like, where sun lands and what you’d rather hide or show off, and the layout follows. Photos of yards you love are a big help.

What plants do well near the coast?

Distance from open water and the amount of sun decide it. Plenty of local yards rely on crotons, agave, palms and bromeliads, but the estimate visit picks plants for your exact bed, not from a generic list.

Do you handle commercial properties as well?

Yes. Commercial properties get the same care as homes, anywhere from Venice up to Parrish in Sarasota and Manatee County. Call or text (239) 887-8922 to arrange a walk-through, and share any HOA or property manager rules up front.
