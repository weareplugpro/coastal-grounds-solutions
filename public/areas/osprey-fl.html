Osprey // Sarasota County

Built for salt, sand and wind.

On Little Sarasota Bay you’ll find Historic Spanish Point, while inland, Oscar Scherer State Park protects scrub and flatwoods. Casey Key Road runs the full barrier island, reached by the one-lane Blackburn Point bridge or from Albee Road. On the mainland, The Oaks and nearby neighborhoods sit close to the water, linked by US 41.

Plants within about an eighth of a mile of salt water have to handle salt spray, and the sandy, high-pH soil drains so quickly that beds dry out between storms. Natural picks include bougainvillea, wax myrtle, palms and agave, and mulch with drip lines holds moisture longer. A windbreak of sturdy shrubs protects what’s behind it, and native plants already thriving on a coastal lot usually tolerate salt.

As the closest stop, Osprey is about 19 minutes south of downtown Sarasota on US 41, so the crew gets there easily any day of the week.

What plants tolerate salt air near the bay?

The toughest options are usually bougainvillea, wax myrtle, palms, agave and native coastal plants. Softer tropicals belong farther from the water or behind a windbreak. Let us know how close you are to the shore.

Why do my beds dry out so quickly?

Sand drains fast and coastal wind dries it further. Mulch, rock over weed barrier and drip irrigation each slow the loss. New plantings need extra attention all through their first season, even in the rainy months.

Near the water, is a rock front yard worth it?

Often, yes. Wind and heavy rain don’t move rock, it drains well and it needs little water, while fabric underneath keeps weeds down. Gravel, a stepping-stone path and a few palms add up to a clean, low-maintenance front yard.
